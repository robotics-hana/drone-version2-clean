How to read this page

This project recreates the AirVLA paper's aerial pick-and-place system: a π₀ vision-language-action model flying a simulated drone with a two-joint arm. The drone must pick a small object off a table and place it in a box, or fly through a gate and hover over an object. The policy was trained by imitation on 400 scripted demonstrations.

Two ideas organise everything here.

First: the evaluation harness is code, and it was broken. Every early result was measured through a test rig that could not have measured a successful pick. Two cheap tests establish whether a harness is trustworthy: a teacher-forced probe (show the policy training images and check its predicted actions against the expert's — no simulator involved), and a ground-truth replay (feed the expert's own recorded actions through the evaluation rig — if those fail, no policy can pass). "Honest harness" on this page means the repaired rig, which passes ground-truth replay, plus evaluation start positions matched to training. The repair flags are --platfix (repaired arm and grasp logic) and --startfix (matched start positions).

Second: distances are the primary metric, success counts are secondary. The grasp window is about 10 mm. When an intervention brings the typical approach to 30–40 mm, each episode's success becomes close to a coin flip: our two oracle-yaw replicates scored 5/20 and 1/20 successes while their median approach distances differed by only 7 mm on identical scenes. Rates are therefore always quoted with a 95% confidence interval, and no rate below n=40 is treated as a comparison.

Where things stand

The policy has learned the task's structure (probe correlations ≈ 0.9) and navigates well (12/20 honest nav successes at 60k). Picking remains rare: the frozen n=60 baseline measures 3/60 pick-ups (5%, CI ≈ 1–14%) at median 242 mm — on video these are 2 sustained carries and 1 momentary grab (`picked` latches on one tick of weld + 12 cm lift). The overnight experiment set completed on 31 Aug: pinning the heading collapses the miss 6.3× (38.6 mm at n=40); handing the policy the exact target vector helps far less (168 mm) even though an attendance control proves the feature is used; training to 90k retired "train longer" by the pre-registered rate criterion; and a 2×2 language probe showed the prompt noun is behaviourally inert — and a dataset test (31 Aug) shows the training data never let position stand in for language (best position rule: 52.3%, chance), so the inert prompt is a model/training failure, not a collector flaw. The consistent picture: information and training scale are not the binding constraint — converting known target position into flight, dominated by the heading channel, is. Only the slot-swap arm (Monday) remains running.

The instrument: proved broken, then proved fixed

Question

Could the evaluation rig have detected a successful pick at all?

What was run

Method
snapshot the full simulator state at the first expert tick of a demonstration, restore it exactly, drive the evaluation platform with the expert's recorded actions
Episodes
3 (two weight, one plush penguin), all successful in the collector
Harness
broken (before), repaired (after)

Result

Ground-truth replayBefore repairAfter repair (fixfit5)
episodes placed in box0/33/3
closest jaws-to-target102–152 mm3.3–6.5 mm
final object vs box centretowed off the table / 2 m away7–13 mm
grasp-weld timing vs expertwrong moment or neverwithin 4 control ticks

What it means

Before the repair, the expert's own actions failed — so no policy could have scored a pick, and every earlier pick number measured the rig. Five independent defects were found, each alone sufficient for 0/20: a stale arm-deployment threshold from an earlier scene geometry; a grasp weld that could trigger on empty air and then drag the object; evaluation start altitudes that overlapped training starts in 0 of 225 episodes; an action clip 14% tighter than the collector's; and a contact check defeated by physics-solver flicker on the plush toy. The repair was accepted only when ground-truth replay passed.

What this does not show

A passing replay proves the rig is fair to the expert's trajectory family; it does not prove the rig is fair to every strategy a policy might try. One earlier replay attempt was itself struck for re-drawing scene randomisation, which is why the byte-faithful snapshot matters.

What it means

The policy tracks the object's lateral position partially — it closes about 40% of the offset — and flies the remainder toward the training average. The information needed to do better exists in the observation: the object stays inside the nose camera's view for the whole approach, though thinly (18 px median at a metre). The heading-sign test turned out to be non-discriminating: a policy flying to a fixed point produces the same 18/20 sign opposition as a failed aiming loop, because both derive from where the object sits relative to the destination.

What this does not show

None of these are interventions; they localise the deficit but cannot assign cause. The camera table is measured on expert approaches — a policy flying elsewhere may see less. An earlier version of this analysis was discarded for three methodology faults (positional episode selection, airframe-instead-of-jaws distances, world-frame "lateral"); this version is the corrected one, and the two attribution retractions it forced are recorded in decision log entry D47.

What it means

Replanning more often did not help; it roughly doubled the median miss. This was the pre-registered favourite hypothesis (decision log D48), and it lost. What it means

Pinning the heading collapsed the median miss by roughly a factor of eight in both replicates, and produced the only pick-ups of the project. This is the largest effect any intervention has produced. The success-count swing between replicates (5 vs 1) against a 7 mm paired distance difference is the page's standing example of why distances are the primary metric.

What this does not show

It does not isolate a mechanism. The pin changes three things at once: the aiming itself; the view — both the nose camera and the arm-mounted wrist camera swing to face the object, giving the policy training-like imagery; and the geometry — the forward-reaching gripper is placed on the drone-to-object line by construction, which reduces lateral miss mechanically. This run cannot separate those three. Separately, the disappearance of the 120 mm fore–aft undershoot under the pin is measured but unexplained — an 18° heading error on a 0.2 m gripper lever accounts for about 10 mm of it, not 120. The v1 run's 0/20 successes despite 5 pick-ups is an artifact of its own design: the always-on pin froze the carry heading once the object was held.

Oracle-vector — hand the policy the exact target vector

Question

If the policy is given the true gripper-to-target vector as three extra state inputs, does everything downstream of perception work?

What was run

build_oracle.py (dataset: vector derived by forward kinematics from the logged scene state — no new episodes flown), then a 10,000-step fine-tune of the 60k checkpoint, then eval_frozen_v1.py --oraclestate

20 picks per arm
Seeds
scene 77000, torch 1000
Harness
repaired, frozen protocol

Result

MetricTrue vectorCorrupted vector (control)
median miss (IQR)168.1 mm (54–232)306.9 mm (178–396)
picked up1/20 (CI 0.1–25%)0/20
paired degradation, corrupt − true+145.8 mm median (attendance bar: +80 mm) · worse in 13/20
distribution shape (true)bimodal: 9/20 episodes under 80 mm, heavy tail to 680 mm

What it means

The attendance control passes: corrupting the vector's direction costs 146 mm, so the model learned to use the feature within the 10,000-step fine-tune. That makes the true-vector result interpretable under the pre-registration, and it reads on the "perception was never the whole constraint" side: even attending to the exact gripper-to-target vector, the policy reaches only 168 mm median and one pick-up. Set beside oracle-yaw — pinning the heading achieves 38.6 mm — providing the information helps far less than acting on it, which points at the action side, dominated by the heading channel.

What this does not show

The oracle vector is privileged information that no deployed system would have; this is a diagnostic bound, not a capability. Attendance is partial — the bimodal true-arm distribution suggests the 10k fine-tune integrated the new feature in only about half the episodes, so 168 mm is an upper bound on the deficit, not a converged measurement.

Slot swap — nose camera into the base input training

Question

π₀'s pretraining treats its base-camera input as the always-present spatial reference and its wrist inputs as auxiliary. Ours feeds the base slot a room-wide view carrying ~2–4 pixels of target. Does swapping the informative nose camera into the base slot recover picking?

What was run

Training
full retrain from π₀ base, batch 8 on Sparks (jobs 859/860), identical to the 30k-b8 control except: camera2→base_0_rgb, camera3→wrist slot, and a 40-episode holdout excluded (the project's first). Started 2026-08-30 16:55; due Monday.
Eval
frozen protocol, honest harness, vs the 30k-b8 control

Result

Pending (Monday).

What this does not show

Two things change at once (slot mapping and holdout), so a difference from the control is not attributable to the swap alone — this arm was deliberately demoted after review; it runs because the hardware was otherwise idle. The mechanism it tests is a learned prior, which fine-tuning can in principle override — 240,000 samples not having overridden it is itself weak evidence against this arm.
