# v4-style pipeline: what each file is for, and why

Everything here layers onto `collect_demos.py` by patching its functions at
**runtime**. `collect_demos.py` is never edited. The only edited model file is
`vla_drone.xml` — an editable, self-contained copy of `SkyGrip_full.xml` that
adds three purely visual hazard placards and two re-aimed cameras.

## Why this exists rather than `collect_v4.py`

The cluster's v4 layer (`~/Drone-Arm_VLA/.../collect_v4.py` and its
`collect_demos.py` lineage) no longer produces working ACT episodes. Measured
on its **own stock pilot**, unmodified, before any of this was written:

| metric | measured | spec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| act success | **0 / 3** | — |
| refuse_hazard / refuse_ungrounded | 2/2, 1/1 | — |
| gate 1, close index from decision | 40, 42, 43 | ≤ 15 |
| gate 4, jaw-to-target at close | 21 / 563 / 669 mm | ≈ 21 mm |

The drone never arrives over the object before GRASP fires. It is **not** the
spawn (fails identically with the six sectors and with a lateral corridor) and
**not** the v4 timing override (fails with `timing=None`). Refusals still pass,
which is why the breakage is easy to miss — any summary mixing modes looks
healthy. That lineage's `SkyGrip_core.xml` also carries an uncommitted
whole-file 585-line rewrite, so check `git diff` there first if repairing it.

The v4 **design** is therefore reimplemented on the local pipeline, which does
grasp reliably.

## Files

| file | what it does | why it is separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v4style.py` | The episode layer: placards, matched-pair fingerprinting, the shared opening, both endings, the smooth-reference controller, spawn bands, episode draws. | Single source of truth. The demo video and the training data are generated by the *same* code, so what you watch is what you train on. |
| `collect_v4style.py` | Schedule → episodes → `LeRobotDataset` + metadata sidecar. | Collection-only concerns (allocation, LeRobot writing, retries) kept out of the episode logic. |
| `render_v4style_demo.py` | Three-view review video of 5 hand-picked episodes. | Reviewing behaviour is a different job from collecting data; it renders extra cameras and banners that must never touch the dataset. |
| `vla_drone.xml` | Editable model copy: hazard placards + re-aimed wrist/scene cameras. | `SkyGrip_full.xml` and its includes are shared with other experiments and must not change. Verified physics-identical: placards are `contype=0 conaffinity=0 mass=0`, total model mass unchanged to 5 dp. |

## The design, and the measurements behind it

**Pick-and-HOLD, not pick-and-place.** After the lift the drone carries the
object back to a centred hover and holds ≥ 2 s — the *same station a refusal
retreats to*, so the two endings differ by what is in the gripper rather than
by where the aircraft is.

**One shared opening, two endings.** Both modes fly
`HOVER_START → APPROACH → REACH_OUT → DRAW_IN → INSPECT` and diverge only after
a fixed-length dwell. A refusal is not "back off from the start": it flies the
full approach, reaches the inspection pose where the placard resolves in the
wrist camera, dwells, and only then retreats. Without this symmetry a direction
extracted between the two populations encodes **trajectory** ("am I high and
still or low and descending") rather than **decision**. The dwell runs its
budget exactly in both modes, so duration carries no information either.

*Verified*: in the save smoke, both members of pair 23 had `decision_frame=437`
and both members of pair 9 had `decision_frame=416` — identical openings,
divergence only after.

**Smooth reference.** `collect_demos` commands each waypoint as a step, so the
PD controller slams at every phase start. The policy trains on these
trajectories, so that jerk is noise a VLA will reproduce and integrate into
drift. `SmoothRefController` walks a trapezoidal reference to each waypoint
instead. It also measurably improves the grasp: DESCEND arrives with the jaws
**5–7 mm** from the object, against **44 mm** when stepped.

**Settings that are load-bearing — do not "optimise" without re-running these:**

| variant | picks | median episode | peak tilt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reach opening, arm slew 0.10 rad/s | 4/4 | 24.0 s | 2.8° |
| arm slew 0.22 | 0/3 | 40.0 s | 7.6° |
| arm slew 0.35 | 0/3 | 33.9 s | 4.5° |
| single-slew opening (EXTEND+SEEK) | 0/4 | 27.5 s | **71.5°** |

Every "faster" option comes out **slower** in wall clock, because a missed
grasp costs a whole re-grasp retry. Trimming that *did* work: capping GRASP at
2.0 s (it otherwise sits out a 4 s budget with the aircraft parked) and
hold-frames 8 → 5, together 25.9 s → 21.9 s, verified 6/6 at 2.5° peak tilt.

**Not taken (user direction):** v4's six azimuth spawn sectors. The drone is not
spawned around the table; the lateral-corridor start is kept.

## Dataset allocation (730 episodes)

    train    480 = 120 matched pairs (240) + 24 act w/ placard on distractor
                   + 96 placard-free act + 120 refuse-ungrounded
                 → 240 act / 120 refuse_hazard / 120 refuse_ungrounded (50/25/25)
                 → P(placard | act) = 144/240 = 0.60
    eval     160 = 40 novel colour + 40 novel spawn + 30 pairs (60) + 20 varied placard
    recover   90 = 45 act / 22 refuse_hazard / 23 refuse_ungrounded at stuck states

`P(placard | act) = 0.60` is the point of the tag marginal: "approach the
unplacarded object" then solves only 60 % of act scenes, so it is not a viable
shortcut and the policy has to key on *which* object is placarded.

**Held-out novel spawn.** The spec's radial band only means something with the
six sectors. The lateral-corridor equivalent, chosen from the numbers:
training starts are always `|x| ≤ 0.12` while objects reach `|x| ≤ 0.225`, so
training **never** starts the drone outside the object row. The held-out band
is `|x| ∈ U(0.26, 0.38)`, either side — clearing the outermost object by 35 mm.
Depth and altitude keep their training distributions, so exactly one factor
changes. *Verified usable*: 8/8 expert picks, peak tilt 2.6° vs 2.7° for
training starts, traverse 5–53 cm vs 1–27 cm. A held-out failure is therefore
the policy failing to generalise, not the expert failing to fly.

**Ungrounded episodes** name a colour absent from the scene **entirely**. The
v4 spec used a colour lure (named colour present on the wrong shape), which is
defensible — refusing correctly then requires joint binding — but reads as an
error on screen when an object of the named colour is plainly sitting there.
The *shape* lure is kept, so shape alone still does not ground the instruction.

## The metadata sidecar

`<repo>_meta.json` is **not** optional bookkeeping. LeRobot frames carry only
the task string, but direction extraction must select populations by mode,
relation and band, and must know each episode's **decision frame**. Both are
recorded per episode, along with the scene fingerprint, placard pixels at the
decision frame, and the tag condition.

Matched pairs are banked **atomically**: both members succeed and their
fingerprints match, or neither is written. A half-pair would silently break
every paired analysis downstream.
